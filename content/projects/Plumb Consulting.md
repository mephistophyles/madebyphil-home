Plumb is my consulting practice for AI strategy and implementation. Not a chatbot mill, not a slide deck factory — senior, hands-on help for teams who are past the demo and need something that survives production.

## The Problem

Every company has an AI initiative now, and most of them are stuck in the same place: a flashy demo that impressed the board, and no credible path from there to something reps or customers actually rely on. Leadership doesn't know which projects are worth the spend and which are hype. Teams don't know who to hire or what "done" looks like. And most consultants either sell a strategy deck nobody implements, or a black-box delivery nobody can inspect afterward.

## The Solution

I work as the senior operator a team doesn't have yet — sizing what's actually worth building, then either building it with them or handing off a system they can run themselves. Every engagement ships with a full public case study: architecture, prompts, evals, and the decisions behind them, client data scrubbed but the method left in the open. You can read exactly how the work gets built before you commit a dollar.

## The Offer

- **Fractional & Retained Advisory** — a senior operator on call for architecture reviews, hiring help, and vendor sanity-checks, with fair-use terms set together up front
- **AI Strategy** — a prioritized roadmap in plain language, sized to the team, that holds up in front of both the board and the engineers
- **Special AI Projects** — experiments or delivery work, run and fully documented
- **Workshops** — half- or full-day sessions to build a working intuition for AI decisions, aimed at executives and business owners rather than engineers
- Every engagement produces a published case study — the real architecture and real numbers, not a testimonial

## What I Learned

The hardest part of positioning this wasn't the offer, it was the proof. Anyone can claim "pragmatic, not hype-driven" — the case library exists so a prospect doesn't have to take my word for it. Writing up the knowledge-pipeline build (Mimir) as the first case taught me that the most convincing case study isn't a client logo, it's a real system with the messy middle — the tuning, the bugs, the things that didn't work on the first pass — left in.
