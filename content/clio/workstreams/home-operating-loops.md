The first home loop is weekly dinner planning. It is deliberately practical: three cooking nights, leftovers the rest of the week, BJ's bulk shopping, simple ingredients, and meals that support exercise and weight loss without turning dinner into a second job.

The interesting part is not the recipes. Recipes are cheap. The hard part is making the loop fit the household:

- two adults with limited evening energy;
- two young kids who may or may not eat the adult meal;
- a severe shellfish allergy;
- a preference for simple food that still has variety;
- shopping that often happens after the kids are asleep;
- feedback from both Phil and his wife, not just one person's theory of what should work.

The current design keeps Clio out of the decision-maker role. Clio gathers constraints, checks whatever BJ's data is available, offers one to three options, and prints the grocery list only after the household chooses. Feedback becomes part of the next plan.

This workstream is a useful test of the collaboration because the stakes are ordinary. If Clio can reduce friction around dinner without creating more management overhead, the same pattern may transfer to other parts of home life.
